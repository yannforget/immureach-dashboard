# ECV 2026

The 2026 round of the *Enquête de Couverture Vaccinale* (ECV) arrived as
`data/input/ecv/ECV2026_Vac_Palu_VR12HFC1_WKF2.dta`, alongside the 2022 and
2023 exports the pipeline already consumes. This document records what is in
that file, how it differs from the two earlier rounds, what the differences
mean for the three `scripts/process-ecv-*.py` scripts, and what the headline
indicators look like when they are computed on it.

Nothing in the pipeline reads the 2026 file yet. The figures below were
computed ad hoc to characterise the data; see
[How these numbers were produced](#how-these-numbers-were-produced) for the
caveat that makes them provisional.

## Fieldwork period

**ECV 2026 was collected in April and May 2026** — interviews run from
**11 April to 31 May 2026**, with submissions arriving through 1 June. Three
independent columns agree:

| Source                              | Evidence                                            |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| `SubmissionDate`                    | 2026-04-11 → 2026-06-01; 26,058 April, 49,095 May, 46 on 1 June |
| `q117` (date de début de l'interview) | 38,691 April 2026, 36,366 May 2026                |
| `today` / `current_year` + `current_month` | 36,448 April, 38,720 May; `current_year` = 2026 on 75,168 of 75,199 rows |

Weekly interview volume, from `today` — the ramp-up, plateau and tail of a
real fieldwork calendar:

```
Apr 06–12        976
Apr 13–19      9,398
Apr 20–26     16,107
Apr 27–May 03 17,694
May 04–10     17,005
May 11–17     10,550
May 18–24      3,272
May 25–31        156
```

All 24 provinces were surveyed in parallel inside that window — the earliest
province starts between 10 and 18 April, the latest finishes 31 May — so
there is no staggered regional rollout to account for when comparing
provinces.

### Decoding the date columns

`today`, `start` and `end` are stored as **strings holding seconds since
1582-10-15**, the Gregorian reform date. Read naively they parse as the year
2413, or fail outright:

```python
EPOCH = pd.Timestamp("1582-10-15")
today = EPOCH + pd.to_timedelta(pd.to_numeric(df["today"]), unit="s")
```

In the 2022 and 2023 files the same three columns are additionally scaled by
1/10, so they need `× 10` before the conversion. `SubmissionDate` and `q117`
are ordinary Stata datetimes in every round and need no conversion at all;
prefer them.

31 rows (0.04%) carry stray `today` / `q117` values — 2017, October–November
2025, March 2026, and a handful in 2027–2029. These are device-clock errors:
every one of those rows has a `SubmissionDate` inside the normal window.

### The round labels are not the fieldwork years

Applying the same three columns to all three files:

| File label | Actual fieldwork    | Rows in the two main months |
| ---------- | ------------------- | --------------------------- |
| ECV 2022   | **March–April 2023**| 67,859 + 12,070 (`q117`)    |
| ECV 2023   | **April–May 2024**  | 65,611 + 17,744 (`q117`)    |
| ECV 2026   | **April–May 2026**  | 38,691 + 36,366 (`q117`)    |

The first two rounds were fielded a year after the year they are named for;
2026 was not. The real spacing is therefore **about 13 months** between ECV
2022 and ECV 2023, and **about 24 months** between ECV 2023 and ECV 2026 —
not the 1-year and 3-year gaps the labels imply.

This matters for the dashboard's year selector. The labels are safe as
ordered categories, which is how the dashboard uses them today; they are
wrong as positions on a time axis, and wrong as the basis of any
annualised-change calculation.

## Four differences that block a straight re-run

### 1. The weight column is renamed and rescaled

2026 uses `PonderationG`; `ponderation` does not exist. The scale also
changed:

| Round | Column          | Sum       | Mean   | Min  | Max    |
| ----- | --------------- | --------- | ------ | ---- | ------ |
| 2022  | `ponderation`   | 8,702,214 | 106.86 | 4.92 | 632.13 |
| 2023  | `ponderation`   | 8,869,936 | 106.34 | 5.44 | 659.51 |
| 2026  | `PonderationG`  | 788,191   | 10.48  | 1.07 | 54.56  |

Proportions are unaffected — every indicator the pipeline publishes is a
ratio — but any weighted *count* built off 2026 is on a different scale and
is not comparable to the earlier rounds without rescaling.

### 2. Geography is coded, not named

The earlier rounds store names the pipeline can clean and match
(`"kl Kwilu Province"`, `"kl Bagata Zone de Santé"`). 2026 stores identifiers:

```
q101   KL
q103   KL_KL_BAGATA
q105   KL_KL_BAGATA_KL_MOSANGO
```

`clean_name()` leaves these untouched, so canonicalisation against
`zones.geojson` drops every row. The fix is mechanical: take the zone name as
the part of `q103` after the second underscore, replace the remaining
underscores with spaces (56 of the 448 zone names contain one, e.g.
`BENA_DIBELE`), and map the two-letter province code to its name. With that,
**448 of 448 zone names match `zones.geojson`** — `normalize()` in the
processing scripts is already case- and accent-insensitive, so the uppercase,
unaccented spelling is not itself a problem.

Two zone names each occur in two provinces (`LUBUNGA`, `BILI`), which is why
the scripts key zones on `province | zone` rather than the name alone.

Province code crosswalk, derived by matching each code's zone-name set
against the 2023 file (every code matched its province on all but four zones,
which are genuinely new — see below):

| Code | Province       | Code | Province       | Code | Province     |
| ---- | -------------- | ---- | -------------- | ---- | ------------ |
| BU   | Bas Uele       | KG   | Kwango         | MG   | Mongala      |
| EQ   | Equateur       | KL   | **Kwilu**      | MN   | Maniema      |
| HK   | Haut Katanga   | KN   | Kinshasa       | NU   | Nord Ubangi  |
| HL   | Haut Lomami    | KR   | Kasai Central  | SN   | **Sankuru**  |
| HU   | Haut Uele      | KS   | **Kasai**      | SU   | Sud Ubangi   |
| IT   | Ituri          | LL   | Lualaba        | TN   | Tanganyika   |
| KC   | Kongo Central  | LM   | **Lomami**     | TP   | Tshopo       |
| KE   | Kasai Oriental | MD   | Maindombe      | TU   | Tshuapa      |

All four dashboard provinces (bold) are present, with sample sizes in line
with 2023.

### 3. The age window means something different

`vs25` spans 6–35 months in 2026, but the round targeted **12–23 months**:

| Round | `vs25` span | 6–24 months | 12–23 months | under 12 | over 24 |
| ----- | ----------- | ----------- | ------------ | -------- | ------- |
| 2022  | 6–23        | 81,438      | 47,880       | 33,558   | 0       |
| 2023  | 0–36        | 83,372      | 48,326       | 35,082   | 5       |
| 2026  | 6–35        | 74,230      | **73,104**   | 984      | 969     |

The scripts default to `AGE_MIN_MONTHS = 6` / `AGE_MAX_MONTHS = 24`, which is
close to a no-op on 2022 and 2023 because those rounds *are* 6–23 month olds.
On 2026 the same window keeps a population that is almost entirely 12–23
months old. Comparing the three rounds at the default window therefore
compares different age structures, which matters most for the antigens given
late in the schedule — see the warning under
[Antigen coverage](#antigen-coverage).

A per-year age window is the cleanest fix; restricting all three rounds to
12–23 months is the alternative, at the cost of halving the 2022 and 2023
samples.

### 4. Two provinces are missing, and a few zones moved

Nord Kivu and Sud Kivu were not surveyed in 2026. National figures for 2026
are therefore national over 24 provinces, and are not strictly comparable to
the 26-province 2022 and 2023 national figures.

Zone-level changes against 2023, all of which already exist in
`zones.geojson`:

- **New in 2026**: Damas, Drodro and Lolwa (Ituri); Lowa (Tshopo).
- **Gone in 2026**: Nia Nia (Ituri).

## Data-quality checks

Three checks were run on the 2026 file; all pass.

- **No missing antigen data.** Every one of the 17 `_merg` columns (plus
  `vpi2`, `var2`, `vap1`–`vap4`) is non-missing for all 74,230 children in
  the 6–24 window, and carries only the codes 1 and 2. The scripts' "unknown
  as soon as one column is missing" rule therefore excludes nobody.
- **Derived zero-dose agrees with the file's own flag.** The pipeline's
  definition — all 17 core `_merg` columns equal 2 — matches `dose0_1 == 1`
  for **99.97%** of children, better than 2022 (99.59%) and 2023 (98.54%).
- **Weights are complete.** `PonderationG` has no missing values, so the
  `dropna(subset=["weight"])` step in `load_year()` drops nothing.

## What it would take to add 2026 to the pipeline

The three scripts share the same shape, so the same four changes apply to
each of `process-ecv-keys.py`, `process-ecv-vaccination-coverage.py` and
`process-ecv-caracteristics.py`:

1. **Add `"2026"` to `YEARS`** and widen the input glob — the 2026 file is
   named `ECV2026_*`, without the underscore the `ECV_{year}_*.dta` pattern
   expects.
2. **Make the weight column per-year**: `PonderationG` for 2026,
   `ponderation` otherwise.
3. **Make geography parsing per-year**: for 2026, split `q103` / `q105` on
   `_`, restore spaces in the names, and map the `q101` code to a province
   name via the crosswalk above. Everything downstream — `normalize()`,
   `canonicalize_names()`, the `province | zone` keys — then works unchanged.
4. **Make the age window per-year**, so 2026 is read as a 12–23 month round
   and the earlier rounds keep their 6–23 population.

`process-ecv-caracteristics.py` needs one thing more: 13 of its 24 variables
have no 2026 source. `Indicator.source(year)` already returns `None` when a
year has no entry, so the mechanism exists — but the per-year source maps
need explicit 2026 entries only for the 11 variables that survive, and the
output rows for 2026 will carry empty `_pct` / `_low` / `_high` triplets for
the rest. The chart and `ECV_CARACTERISTIC_GROUPS` have to handle a year with
missing variable groups rather than assume every root is present in every
year.

Two decisions are not ours to make silently and should be settled first:

- **Two fewer provinces.** Whether a 2026 national figure over 24 provinces
  may sit in the same series as a 26-province 2022/2023 figure.
- **A different age group.** Whether to publish 2026 on its own 12–23 month
  window, or to re-cut all three rounds to 12–23 for comparability.

## How these numbers were produced

Every percentage in this document is a **weighted point estimate computed in
pandas**, as `Σ(weight × indicator) / Σ(weight)` over the children with a
known answer. They are *not* the design-based estimates the pipeline
publishes: `process-ecv-*.py` shells out to R's `survey` package and reports
`svyciprop(method = "logit")` point estimates with 95% confidence intervals,
accounting for stratification and clustering. `Rscript` was not available in
the environment where this exploration was run.

The point estimates will be very close — the weighting is the same — but they
are not identical, and this document carries **no confidence intervals at
all**. Zone-level figures in particular are meaningless without them: a zone
contributes 4–8 aires de santé and a few hundred children. Treat everything
here as a characterisation of the file, and regenerate the published numbers
through the pipeline once 2026 is wired in.
